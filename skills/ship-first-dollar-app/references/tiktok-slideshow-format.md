# TikTok slideshow format

Read this before writing TikTok photo slideshow posts in step 6.
Source: one founder's analysis, published 2026-10-17, of 13,196 slideshows from 580 app-promoting accounts, 2026 only: <https://x.com/ericcosulea/status/2112214949736898809>.
Every number is a change in views against the same account's other posts.
No installs, retention or revenue were measured.
Treat each row as a hypothesis to test on your own account.
The dataset is not public, the labels came from an LLM, and the author reports no label accuracy check.
The author's own summary is that most gains are 10 to 20 percent and not 10x.

## Hook (slide 1)

- Use a list: "[number] [things] that [result]". +16%.
- Use 5, 6 or 7 items. 6 is +37% on 340 posts, 5 is +16% on 1,752, 7 is +15%.
  Avoid 3 items (-23%).
- Speak as "I" (+7%).
  Avoid "you" (-9%).
- Add a second line in brackets under the hook with a result or a teaser (+19%).
- Keep the hook at 12 words or fewer. 13 or more is -6%.
- A recognizable celebrity or character on slide 1 is +48% but only 262 posts.
- Questions (-23%) and quotes (-19%) lose.

## Body

- Every slide gives one useful item.
  Tips lists +34%, app lists +39%.
  One-message posts -30%.
- Number the slides "1.", "2.", "3." (+21%, against -17% without).
- Aim for 8 or more slides. 10 or more +23%. 1 to 3 slides -19%.
- Put text on every slide.
  Text only on slide 1 is -19%.
- Break text into short lines. 7 or more lines per slide +12%, 1 to 2 lines -9%.
- Use specific numbers in tips, such as minutes or counts (+10%).
- Skip emoji on slides (+6% without).

## The app

- Show no app screenshot (+29%).
  A feature screenshot is -11% and an App Store page is -26%.
- Keep the app out of the first 4 slides (-14% to -19%).
  Later is better, with the caveat that long posts also win.
- Put the app inside an app list when it fits (+39%).

## Image source

- Make the slide art yourself or with a tool you hold the rights to, not other people's images from Pinterest or elsewhere.
  TikTok's Community Guidelines say "You should only post content you created or have the right to share", and an app that reuses others' art carries that risk.
  A reply makes the same point about takedown email risk (single report).
- One growth-account owner claims cartoon art is "super hard to identify as ai", so viewers do not object and it is more trustworthy and easier to remember (single claim, no data).
  The example they showed is a mascot account, `quabble_app`, with 9,287 followers, 208.7K likes and a recurring duck on every slide.
  The view counters are all the evidence, so no install, retention or comment data was shown.
  Test one recurring character against your usual slides on your own account before building a library around it.
- TikTok requires a label on AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people.
  Flat cartoon art is not obviously in that group (inference), but the guidelines do not define the edge, so label anything that looks photographic.
  TikTok also reads C2PA Content Credentials metadata and adds invisible watermarks to content uploaded with credentials.
- One owner advises stripping metadata so TikTok cannot detect AI (single claim, not confirmed by any TikTok page read).
  Do not do it, because TikTok reads that metadata.
  Stripping it would likely hide realistic AI content that the label rule covers (inference).

## Ending and caption

- Do not end on "download", "link in bio" or `try <app>` (-21%).
- End on the last tip, or on "save this + follow for more" (+101% on 174 posts across 24 accounts).
- Use 4 to 6 hashtags (+14%).
  None is -26%.
- Write at least one real sentence of caption, 6 words or more.

## Cadence

One founder claims one post a day beats ten posts once (October 2026, no data, no comparison run, and the author promotes TikTok tooling).
Treat it as a hypothesis to test on your own account.

## Counter-evidence to keep next to the table

- One creator measured reach by slide: a 5-slide post reached 100%, 66%, 51%, 37% of viewers by slide 4 and 29% at the CTA slide, and a 7-slide post had only 10% reach the CTA.
  Late CTAs and late app slides lose viewers.
- One creator reported a download CTA drove 15 to 20 times more downloads than no CTA in a single test.
  The study measured views only.
- Several creators report posts with 300,000 views and 0 installs.
- The author's own 10K-view post changed the hook, added a save and follow ending and changed how the app is shown, so the lift cannot be assigned to one change.
- Mass offshore slideshow farms: see [distribution-channels.md](distribution-channels.md) for bot-view and spam-policy cautions.

## Labels worth recording per post

Seen in the author's dashboard frame, so the field list is observed.
Whether the fields predict anything is not shown.

- Hook: format, who it speaks to, number promised, words, the promise, a bracketed second line.
- Slides: count, numbered or not, words per slide, max lines, text style and position, point of view, emoji, concrete numbers, text on slide 1 only.
- Photos: slide 1 type (celebrity, screenshot, group, face visible, object), vibe, aspect ratio.
- Image source: own illustration, AI-generated, licensed stock, or other people's images, and whether a recurring character appears.
  This field is not in the dashboard frame the list came from, so it is a proposed field and not an observed one.
- App: promoted or not, slide number, how it is shown (none, a feature, the store page), how it is pitched.
- Ending and caption: CTA type, caption words, hashtag count, question in the caption, sound, weekday.
- Result: views divided by the account's usual views.
