# Screenshot copy and design

Adapted from ParthJadhav/app-store-screenshots (MIT, <https://github.com/ParthJadhav/app-store-screenshots>, commit 5386f22).
The contrast and headline-fit rules come from its `style-prompts/_QUALITY_BAR.md`, and the added copy formulas come from its `copy-ideas.md`.

The value-prop research step, the deck-level outcome, and the human element default are adapted from a screenshot studio's article (<https://x.com/scrnshtstudio/status/2090816024585089129>).
The article gives no conversion data, so treat them as defaults to A/B test with Product Page Optimization and not as measured rules.

Read this before capturing and framing screenshots.
Copy is decided first, then captures are chosen to prove each headline, then framing is applied.

## 1. Core principle

Screenshots are advertisements, not documentation.
Every screenshot sells one idea.
Sell a feeling, an outcome, or the death of a pain point.
Do not tour the UI.

Before drafting any headline, write the value prop in one sentence from three inputs: who the user is, the pain they have today, and what competing listings promise.
Take the pain and the competitor claims from reviews, the store listings of the top 3 alternatives, or the owner's own research.
If none exists, ask the human and do not invent them.

## 2. Copy rules

### Iron rules

1. One idea per headline. Never join two things with "and".
2. Use short, common words of 1-2 syllables. Avoid jargon unless it is domain-specific.
3. Write 3-5 words per line. The headline must read at App Store thumbnail size.
4. Place line breaks on purpose. A newline in the copy is a visible break in the frame.

### Three approaches

Offer the user three options per slide, one of each type, then let them pick.

| Type             | What it does                  | Example                                      |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Paint a moment   | You picture yourself doing it | "Check your coffee without opening the app." |
| State an outcome | What life looks like after    | "A home for every coffee you buy."           |
| Kill a pain      | Name a problem and destroy it | "Never waste a great bag of coffee."         |

After the user picks, rewrite the chosen line in the voice of the selected visual style.
The same idea reads "Dial in every pour." in a sporty style and "The ritual, refined." in an editorial style.

### Red flags

Rewrite on sight: "seamless", "powerful", "revolutionary", "all-in-one", "next-generation", "leverage", "AI-powered" as the whole message.
Rewrite exclamation marks, questions in headlines, and any headline that still works with a competitor's name swapped in.
Never put "Download now" or a price on a screenshot because the store already shows the button.
Use awards, ratings, press mentions, and user counts only when they are true and permitted.
Apple's asset best-practices page (<https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/>) says: "Please don't include any Apple-designated recognitions - such as Editor's Choice, App of the Day, Game of the Day, or Apple Design Award winner."
It gives the reason that many of these already appear next to the app in search results and on the product page.
The same page says to avoid claims that can't be verified, such as awards or recognition the app hasn't received.
A "#1" or "best" claim falls under the 2.3 rules on unverifiable claims, and no Apple rule names "#1" explicitly.
Earnings promises and money figures on a screenshot ("Earn $40-$500 per day", "+$4,330") need a stated basis you can show on request.
A mock figure must look like sample data and not a promise.
Apple's guidelines as fetched have no earnings-specific rule, so the relevant text is 2.3.1(a), which treats misleading marketing as grounds for removal.
"100% FREE TO USE" and "NO LIMITS" are price and terms claims.
Apple 2.3.7 says screenshots "should not include prices, terms, or descriptions that are not specific to the metadata type", so keep price words in the store price field and keep "no limits" only where it is literally true.
Evidence: Apple text verified through a fetch tool on a partial page, application to the slides in one agency-deck post (October 2026) is inference.

### Eyebrow labels

An eyebrow is the small line above the headline.
Keep it to 1-3 words.
It orients and never carries the benefit.

- Positional: `FEATURE 01`, `STEP 2`, `01 / 05`
- Category: `WIDGETS`, `PRIVACY`, `OFFLINE`, `SYNC`
- Temporal: `EVERY MORNING`, `IN 3 SECONDS`, `TONIGHT`

## 3. Formulas by slot

Pick the deck slot first, then a formula, then fill it with the app's real nouns and verbs.
Never ship a formula with placeholder words left in.

### Hero (the one slide most people see)

| Formula                                 | Example                              |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `[outcome], without [pain].`            | Great coffee, without the guesswork. |
| `The [adjective] way to [verb] [noun].` | The calm way to plan your week.      |
| `Your [noun], [benefit].`               | Your money, finally clear.           |
| `[verb] [noun]. [verb] [noun].`         | Track habits. Keep streaks.          |
| `Meet your new [role].`                 | Meet your new sleep coach.           |
| `Every [noun], [outcome].`              | Every workout, counted.              |
| `[noun] that [verb]s you.`              | A journal that listens back.         |

### Differentiator (why this app and not the others)

| Formula                                 | Example                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `Only [app] [does unique thing].`       | Only Bloom knows when beans peak.           |
| `[Category] [does X]. [App] [does Y].`  | Timers count down. Bloom coaches each pour. |
| `No [pain]. No [pain]. Just [outcome].` | No ads. No streak guilt. Just progress.     |
| `Built for [specific person].`          | Built for people who read slowly.           |
| `[verb] it once. [outcome] forever.`    | Scan it once. Tracked forever.              |

### Feature (one per slide)

| Formula                              | Example                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| `[verb] [noun] in [time or number].` | Log a meal in 3 seconds.           |
| `Never [pain] again.`                | Never miss a refill again.         |
| `From [before] to [after].`          | From receipt to budget in one tap. |
| `[verb] it. [app] does the rest.`    | Snap the bag. Bloom does the rest. |
| `Right on your [surface].`           | Right on your lock screen.         |
| `See [thing] at a glance.`           | See your whole month at a glance.  |
| `[noun], sorted.`                    | Your shelf, sorted by freshness.   |

### Proof (numbers, trust, social)

| Formula                           | Example                                                |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `[number] [people] [verb] [app].` | 40,000 runners train with Stride.                      |
| `Loved by [specific group].`      | Loved by home baristas.                                |
| `[rating] from [number] reviews.` | 4.9 stars from 12,000 reviews.                         |
| `Private by design.`              | Private by design. Your data stays on device.          |
| `As seen in [publication].`       | Use only when the press mention is real and permitted. |

### Closer (last slide)

| Pattern                           | Example                                      |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Feature wordlist, 4-6 short items | Widgets, Journal, Recipes, Caffeine, Scanner |
| Big quiet statement               | And it's all yours.                          |
| "And so much more" pills          | Dark mode, iCloud sync, Siri, Shortcuts      |
| Identity                          | Made for people who care about the details.  |
| Invitation                        | Your first week starts today.                |

## 6. Deck arc and layout rhythm

### Narrative arc

Every slide serves one deck-level outcome, answered first: what is the user hoping to achieve with this app?
Write it once at the top of `store_assets/README.md` and check each headline against it.

Skip slots that do not fit.

| Slot           | Purpose                                                                            |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1              | Hero or main benefit. It is the only slide most people see, so it must work alone. |
| 2              | Differentiator. What makes the app unique.                                         |
| 3              | Ecosystem: widgets, watch, extensions. Skip if not applicable.                     |
| 4 and on       | Core features, one per slide, most important first.                                |
| Second to last | Trust signal such as "made for people who [X]".                                    |
| Last           | More features as pills or a feature wall. Skip if there are few.                   |

Alternative arcs when the benefit ladder does not fit:

- Problem to solution: name the pain, show the fix, one step per slide, outcome, closer.
- Day in the life: morning moment, midday moment, evening moment, weekly review, closer.
- Numbers-led (finance, fitness, health): one giant number with meaning, each feature anchored to a number, proof number, closer.

### Layout variation

Use these layout ideas generically and map them to Koubou's frame and text options.

- Hero: centered headline with the device anchored at the bottom.
- Device bottom: same composition with a smaller headline.
- Device top: flipped, device above the caption. A good contrast slide.
- Two devices: back and front phones layered.
- No device: a big standalone headline. Use sparingly.
- Split landscape: caption left, device right. Use for tablet landscape, TV, and Mac.

Never repeat the same layout twice in a row.
Use one or two inverted (dark) slides for rhythm when the deck is long enough.
Landscape tablet slides use split landscape and never two devices side by side.
Apple's Guideline 2.3.3 needs more than half of the screenshots for each device to show the app in use, so count no-device slides and feature walls against the rest of the deck.

### iPhone Duo decks

- Treat each display and orientation as its own deck.
- Lead with the inner display in landscape when the app has a real two-pane or wide layout.
- Show at least one capture that only an open Duo can produce, such as two panes, a sidebar, or a wider grid.
- Inner landscape: one-line headline of about 30 characters or fewer across the top, device large below.
- Outer portrait: two short lines at most, large type, one idea per screen.
- Use a folded-plus-open pair once per deck for a moment that carries across states, such as "Close it for today. Open it for the week."
- Write about what the user gets, not the hardware.
- Caption each pane by its job for the user, for example "Your balance, front and centre" and "What's due, at a glance", and not by the device (one demo video from one author, weak).
- Keep Duo devices straight-on. Do not tilt or fake a crease.

## 7. Cross-screen composition

For decks of 5+ slides, plan one tasteful cross-screen moment.
For 8-10 slide decks use at most two.
Short or compliance-heavy decks may use none.
The goal is "these screenshots belong together", not one poster chopped into pieces.

Good patterns:

- An oversized device or mosaic bridges two adjacent screens by 10-30% of its width while each crop still reads as a complete ad.
- A background horizon, gradient, path, or waveform continues across the seam.
- A mascot, object, or chip peeks from one screen into the next as a secondary visual.
- Related slides pair up: problem to solution, before to after, overview to detail.

Bad patterns:

- Splitting headlines, app names, prices, ratings, or critical UI across a seam.
- Centering one giant device on the seam so each crop shows half a device and no benefit.
- Using the effect on every slide.
- Cutting through faces, mascot eyes, or key numbers.
- Clipping that looks accidental. Make every bleed deliberate with scale, shadow, or rotation.

Rules:

- Use adjacent screens only.
- Keep all text fully inside a single screen with safe margins.
- Every exported PNG must still pass the one-second standalone test.
- Review both the zoomed-out strip and each individual screen.

## 8. Visual design principles

Derived from decks such as Superlist, Headspace, CRED, Arc Search, Linktree, and Gentler Streak.

1. **The background is a designed surface and never plain white.** Use a saturated color block, a warm cream such as `#F4F1EC`, a dark navy or near-black, or a gradient. It may shift per slide but must look intentional.
2. **Headlines dominate.** The headline takes roughly the top 30-40% of the canvas. If it cannot be read at thumbnail size without zoom, redesign.
3. **Mix emphasis inside the headline.** Style one word differently with a contrast color, italic script, heavier weight, or underline. Superlist uses script and coral on "your whole day". Headspace sets "less" in orange in "Stress less". Pick one emphasis word per slide.
4. **Add a decorative accent to most slides.** Use squiggles, arrows, sparkles, label badges on the visual, or floating chips with stats that match the shown sample data or a figure you can document, such as "$3,630 earned". Award lockups belong on the hero only. A bare device on a bare background with a bare headline is the default output, so add one accent.
5. **Vary device framing across the deck.** Options are bezelless and minimal (maximum UI legibility), tilted floating with a soft shadow (advertorial), and full visible bezel dead-center (editorial, premium). Mix at least two.
6. **Proof anchors the hero and nothing else.** Concentrate awards, press quotes, star counts, and install counts on slide 1. Spreading them dilutes the proof and the other slides.
   One studio's showcase decks spread proof over two or three slides, but that is one studio's opinion with no conversion data, so the rule stands.
   A second studio's showcase (Kree8, October 2026) does the same, also without data.
   Spreading proof across slides is an A/B variant to test and not the default.
7. **Dense inside the device, sparse outside.** The capture should be a real, dense product screen. Outside the device allow one headline, one visual, one optional sub-line, and one optional badge. No bullet lists, paragraphs, or competing logos.
8. **Break the device parade every 2-3 slides.** Swap in a 3D object, a photographic still, a lifestyle photo, a mascot illustration, a typographic feature wall, or a device mosaic.
   For personal categories such as health, learning, audio, and family, try a person, a face, a hand holding the object, or a real-life situation for the hero or the first break.
   It is a default to A/B test and not a proven rule.
   A slide without a capture still counts against the Guideline 2.3.3 majority above.
   Break the parade on at most every second slide so that more than half show a real screen.
9. **End with a feature wall or a device mosaic.** A feature wall is a vertical list of one-word features set as big type. A mosaic is a grid of bezelless mini-screenshots. Pick one. Do not end on another single-feature hero.
10. **Run the thumbnail test before export.** See the checklist below.
11. **Measure contrast, do not eyeball it.**
    WCAG 2.2 level AA asks 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, which is at least 18pt, or 14pt bold (<https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/>).
    The upstream deck rule holds every headline word, including the emphasis word, to 4.5:1 against the worst pixel under its letters.
    Pale tints of the brand color fail most often.
    Fix a failure with a darker hue, a scrim, or a 2-4px shadow at 30-45% alpha, or move the headline.
12. **Fit the headline.**
    Use three lines at most, and two is better.
    Keep each line under about `canvasWidth / fontSize × 0.45` characters.
    Set display leading at 1.05 or less and tracking slightly negative.
    If one word is wider than 88% of the canvas, break it, shrink it, or hyphenate it.
13. **Mascot-led and 3D-type decks, a pattern to try and not a rule.**
    Two of four agency decks in one agency-deck post (October 2026) share one saturated background family, one hero character or object per slide, stickers and badges overlapping the phone edge, and a real screen on the remaining slides.
    Evidence: image reading of four decks with no performance data.

## 9. Localization copy notes

- Translate the idea, not the words. Rewrite for the target market.
- Rebuild line breaks per locale. German and French run 20-35% longer, so shorten the claim before shrinking the font.
- Keep numbers, units, and currency native to the locale, for example `1.234,5 g`, `€`, and 24-hour time where expected.
- Arabic and Hebrew: write the headline RTL-native and re-check which word carries the emphasis because it usually moves.
- Decide per deck whether to mirror the layout for Arabic and Hebrew, because upstream is inconsistent on it, then check every slide to confirm the result looks intentional.
- Japanese and Chinese: use fewer characters per line, avoid mid-phrase breaks, and use a slightly smaller emphasis scale so kana and kanji stay balanced.
- Never machine-translate the hero headline without a native read.
- Check that landscape one-line headlines still fit on one line in every locale.

## Pre-framing checklist

Shrink each slide to about 160px wide (App Store search-result size) and squint before ticking the thumbnail items.

- [ ] Each slide sells exactly one idea.
- [ ] The hero slide communicates the main benefit within one second.
- [ ] Every headline is readable at 160px wide, with strong contrast against the background.
- [ ] Every headline and emphasis word measures at least 4.5:1 against its background.
- [ ] More than half of the slides show the app in use (Guideline 2.3.3), counted with the slide-by-slide table in `SKILL.md`.
- [ ] You can tell what the app does from the thumbnail alone.
- [ ] Headlines are 3-5 words per line, with intentional line breaks and no red-flag words.
- [ ] No two adjacent slides share the same layout.
- [ ] The deck has at least one contrast (inverted) slide if it has 5+ slides.
- [ ] Backgrounds are designed surfaces, with one emphasis word and one accent per slide.
- [ ] Proof appears on slide 1 only, and the last slide is a feature wall or mosaic rather than another hero.
- [ ] Each capture is a real, dense screen that proves its headline.
- [ ] Every laurel, badge, rating and user-count lockup names something true about this app, and its text is not left over from another template.
- [ ] A spell check has run on every headline and sub-line.
- [ ] Any cross-screen element crosses adjacent screens only and never splits text or critical UI.
- [ ] Every locale's copy fits its layout without clipping or mid-phrase wraps.
- [ ] Hide every headline and sub-line, and the visuals alone still show roughly what the app does.
