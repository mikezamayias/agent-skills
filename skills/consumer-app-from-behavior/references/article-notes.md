## Adaptation choices

- The title claims a study of 438,122 apps. The article body contains no data, method, or findings from that study, so the skill cites no numbers from it.
- The article promotes Shipper as the build tool. The skill is tool-agnostic and defers to the project's own stack.
- The retention signal (100 downloads, 25 active a week later) and the $4.99/week and $29.99/year price shape are the author's heuristics, not measured benchmarks.
- Sections that overlap existing skills (reference capture, paywall placement, funnel measurement) hand off to `mobile-ui-reference`, `paywall-conversion-flow`, and `ship-first-dollar-app` instead of duplicating them.

## Method kept

- Demand before ideas: observe behavior on short-form video, separate the trend from the underlying problem, and mine comments for unprompted "is there an app for this" requests.
- Three formats: tracker, coach, and simple utility.
- Define the one-sentence loop and answer download, 10-second understanding, first win, and return-tomorrow questions before code.
- Tear down 5-10 shipped apps for their decisions, not their pixels.
- Use an LLM to write a spec from the references before building.
- Direct iterations with user-centered questions instead of feature requests.
- For v1, work on onboarding, value-first paywall placement, and adversarial self-testing, then ship.
- Start with iOS, and keep one audience, one problem, one promise, and one loop.
